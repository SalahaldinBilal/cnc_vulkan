# Radar (Minimap)

How the minimap is made, and our sharper one, plus the fog of war in the world. Code: `crates/d3d8/src/radar.rs` (terrain), `radar_layers.rs` (shroud, dots, camera box), `fog.rs` (world fog). EA source (`W3DRadar.cpp`, `Radar.h`, `Radar.cpp`, `WorldHeightMap.cpp`), confirmed in `game.dat` where an address is given.

## How the game makes it

- Nothing is pre-made. When a map loads, W3DRadar::newMap `0x0077b140` calls Radar::newMap `0x00496830` and then W3DRadar::buildTerrainTexture `0x0077b160` (call at `0x0077b155`). refreshTerrain calls it again from `0x0077bc41`.
- Size: `RADAR_CELL_WIDTH` and `RADAR_CELL_HEIGHT`, both 128. Radar::newMap sets `m_xSample` (`+0x20`) and `m_ySample` (`+0x24`) to the map extent (`+0x1334` lo, `+0x1340` hi) times 1/128, so the whole map fits whatever its size. A large map gets one texel per several terrain cells.
- radarToWorld2D `0x00496c50` and radarToWorld `0x00496cd0` (thiscall, radar cell in, world point out, callee pops 8): clamp the cell to 0..127 with immediates (`cmp reg, 0x80`, `mov [cell], 0x7f`), then world = cell times the sample step. radarToWorld adds the ground height.
- buildTerrainTexture (thiscall, TerrainLogic): loops over `+0x1374` by `+0x1378` (texture width and height), fetches the terrain texture's surface (`m_terrainTexture` at `+0x1358`, TextureClass::Get_Surface_Level `0x0084fe40`, which reads the D3D texture at TextureClass `+0x38`) and writes each texel with SurfaceClass::DrawPixel `0x00852c40`. Per texel: a working bridge's radar color, else water (`TheWaterTransparency` `0x00a3cfe8` radar color at `+0x20`, shaded by depth, 3x3 samples), else land: 3x3 neighboring texels averaged, each `TheTerrainVisual->getTerrainColorAt` (vtable `+0x10`) lightened or darkened by its height (`0x0077a810`: up to 95% toward white above the average height, 60% toward black below).
- getTerrainColorAt: W3DTerrainVisual (vtable `0x0096a258`, entry `0x0096a268` is `0x007ab2c0`) passes it to its WorldHeightMap (`+0x18`), WorldHeightMap::getTerrainColorAt `0x00747810`: cell = floor(x * 0.1) + border, clamped, tile index from `+0x44` (shorts, row major, `+0xc` cells wide, `+0x10` high, `+0x14` border, `+0x24` count), source tile `index >> 2` from `+0x60` (0x400 of them), and the tile's 1x1 mip. So each tile type is one flat color, blends and cliffs ignored.
- TileData pixels, BGRA, a tile covering 2x2 cells (the low two bits of the cell's index pick its quarter): 64x64 at `+0x8`, 32 at `+0x4008`, 16 at `+0x5008`, 8 at `+0x5408`, 4 at `+0x5508`, 2 at `+0x5548`, 1 at `+0x5558` (getRGBDataForWidth `0x00781b20`).
- Overlay (unit dots, redrawn every 6 frames) and shroud (per cell alpha) are separate 128x128 textures drawn over the terrain image in the same rectangle. The image is drawn stretched, so its texture size doesn't matter to the draw.

## Our sharper image

- Both calls to buildTerrainTexture go to our wrapper. It runs the game's build as usual, then once more with: `+0x1374`/`+0x1378` set to our size, the sample steps scaled by 128 / size, the eight clamp immediates in radarToWorld2D and radarToWorld raised to the size, and Get_Surface_Level (its call at `0x0077b1fc`) handing out the surface of a texture of ours of that size (the D3D texture at `+0x38` swapped for the call). Everything is put back after. Water, bridges and the height shading are the game's own code at the higher resolution.
- The device then draws our image wherever the game's 128x128 texture is drawn (`TextureData::replacement`). The game writing its texture again (the next build, or reset clearing it) drops the replacement.
- Colors from the terrain textures: during our build, getTerrainColorAt (W3DTerrainVisual's vtable entry, which we take over) samples the cell's own tile quarter at the tile mip that gives about two tile pixels per radar texel, instead of the 1x1 mip. Within the quarter, columns run with world x and rows with world y (from the tile data being stored bottom up, the way `getRawTileData` picks quarters, not yet confirmed visually).
- Settings, "Map and Camera" tab: "Minimap resolution" 128 (the game's) to 2048 (the game's build stops at 1024), default 1024, and "Minimap terrain": map from above (default, see below), terrain textures, or the game's colors. Both take effect with the next map. The log says how long each build took (`radar terrain drawn at 512x512 ...`).
- A map load runs the build two or three times in a row (newMap, then refreshTerrain), measured 0.3 s each at 512 and 1.1 s at 1024. Our second pass is skipped when the game's 128x128 image, the size and the color setting are the same as last time, and the last image is attached again.
- Not done: blend layers (terrain type edges stay per cell), cliffs, and a mid-game refresh (bridges) rebuilding at the larger size costs the same time as at map load.

## The map drawn from above

`crates/d3d8/src/radar_map.rs`, "Minimap terrain: Map from above" (the default). Terrain and water work in game, 1 to 5 ms per draw at 1024. The map file's objects and trees (below) are built, not yet seen in game.

- How the game renders into a texture, from WaterRenderObjClass::renderMirror `0x00758500` (EA source matches): DX8Wrapper::Set_Render_Target_With_Z `0x00839900` (texture, z texture; with none it calls Set_Render_Target with the default depth buffer), WW3D::Begin_Render `0x0083ca10` (cdecl: clear, clear depth, color, alpha, callback; returns non-zero on a lost device), the camera adjusted and CameraClass::Apply `0x00856830` (sets the viewport from the render target's size and loads view and projection), WW3D::Render `0x0083cb60` (scene, camera, ...), WW3D::End_Render `0x0083cfb0` (cdecl, flip), DX8Wrapper::Set_Render_Target `0x00839960` (cdecl: surface, use the default depth buffer; null goes back to the back buffer).
- WW3D::Render builds a RenderInfoClass on the stack (`0x130` bytes, constructor `0x00858080`, thiscall with the camera, nothing to destroy) after calling the camera's vtable `+0x2c`, calls the scene's Render (`+0x50`), then what `0x0083cf70` does on its own (cdecl, render info): TheDX8MeshRenderer (`0xa762d8`) flush `0x0085c040`, the static sort list, SortingRendererClass::Flush `0x00842b60`, Clear_Pending_Delete_Lists `0x0085bc00`.
- CameraClass (`0x38c` bytes, the game's operator new `0x004144a0`, constructor `0x008553e0` thiscall, no arguments; vtable `0x00973288`, `+0x4c` Set_Transform): `+0x94` projection (0 perspective, otherwise orthographic), `+0x98` to `+0xa4` viewport, `+0xa8` to `+0xb4` view plane (in orthographic, the extents in camera space), `+0xbc`/`+0xc0` near and far (Set_Clip_Planes `0x00856d30`), `+0xcc` frustum valid (the projection is rebuilt by `0x008562b0` when it's clear).
- The terrain: HeightMapRenderObjClass (constructor `0x0079b670`, vtable `0x00969978`; BaseHeightMapRenderObjClass `0x00750750` with `0x00968078`, whose Render is the pure call stub) Render is vtable slot 9, `0x0079ce40` (RenderObjClass's own slots start one in, after RefCountClass's). It draws the terrain window, shorelines, blend tiles, roads, props, scorches, bridges, tracks and building bibs, without culling, and the shroud only comes as an extra pass from the scene, so drawing it alone leaves out shroud and units. Trees and water are separate and not drawn yet.
- What we do: when the game builds the radar's terrain, we queue the radar, and at the next W3DDisplay::draw, at its Begin_Render call (`0x0073e194`), first: a render target texture of ours of the chosen size, Set_Render_Target with it, Begin_Render clearing to black, our orthographic camera straight down over the middle of the radar's map extent with the view plane covering it, a RenderInfoClass, the terrain's Render, the flush, End_Render(false), Set_Render_Target(null). The device copies the result upside down (radar rows run with world y) into a texture that the radar's terrain texture shows in its place.
- The terrain's Render reads the scene from the camera's user data (CameraClass `+0x8c`, Get_User_Data is vtable `+0x150` `0x0076bc50`, Set_User_Data `0x00750440`) and checks its custom pass mode (`+0x58c`, 1 is the alpha mask pass) and extra pass mode (`+0x18`). RTS3DScene::Customized_Render sets it to itself right before it renders the terrain, so a camera that never went through the scene has none. The first version left it empty and crashed at the shell map with the game's "Technical Difficulties" box. We copy it from the tactical view's 3D camera (W3DView `+0x84`) and wait for a frame where it is set.
- The terrain passes leave the target's alpha mostly at 0 (83% of the shell map's pixels), and the radar draws its terrain texture with alpha, so the first image that made it to the radar showed the dark window behind it. The copy the radar shows is an X8R8G8B8 texture, which the device samples with alpha 1.
- Water: TheWaterRenderObj `0x00a48b40` (created in W3DTerrainVisual's init `0x007aa820`, which adds `water + 4` to the scene `0x00a40474`). Its RenderObjClass part is at `+4` with vtable `0x009682cc`, Render slot 9 `0x00758a60`: skips like the terrain on the scene's pass modes, then by the water type (`+0xe4` from the RenderObjClass part) draws the polygon trigger water (0 and 3, `0x0075a1b0`), the sea with its reflection (2), and then the sky box (at `+0x218` from that part) whenever GlobalData `+0x164` isn't 0.0 (`[0x00943e9c]`), placed at height `+0x160`. We draw it right after the terrain with `+0x164` held at 0 so no sky box covers the map.
- The shroud: every water draw path (drawSea `0x00759f37`, the water mesh `0x0075b461`, the river and trapezoid water `0x0075c960`, `0x0075cb1e`, `0x0075dfb9`) multiplies W3DShroud's texture in as a second pass when TheTerrainRenderObject `+0x2844` isn't null. The map is drawn at load, when the shroud is black everywhere not yet seen, so on a campaign map a river stayed black on the radar after it was explored. `+0x2844` is held at null for the whole draw. Every other reader either checks for null or belongs to construction and teardown, and the terrain itself only draws the shroud as an extra pass the scene asks for.
- When: the radar is built early in startNewGame (newMap, before the map's objects and trees exist), so the draw waits for a frame where TheGameLogic (`0x00a39b0c`) `+0x51`, the loading-map flag startNewGame sets at `0x0049fba0` and clears at its end, is 0.
- The terrain renderer only holds the window it was loaded with, so this needs the whole map loaded (DrawEntireTerrain, the same as the zoom out, and from the next map on). Without it, the textured build is used. The device also leaves out a depth buffer smaller than the target (D3D8 allowed it, Vulkan doesn't), since the default one may be smaller than a 2048 target.

### The map file's objects and trees ("Minimap objects and trees", on by default)

`crates/d3d8/src/map_objects.rs`. Drawn from the map file, not from what's alive: the file's object list stays loaded for the whole game and is the same for a new game and a loaded save (EA source: startNewGame loads the map and walks the list in both cases, a save only uses it for trees, bridges and wall pieces), so the result is the map as it was made, whatever got built or destroyed since.

- The list: TheMapObjectListPtr `0x00a488d0`. MapObject (EA `MapObject.h`, vtable at 0): location `+4` (x, y, z above ground), template `+0x14` (read through getThingTemplate `0x00743880`, thiscall), angle `+0x18`, next `+0x1c`, flags `+0x20` (`0x36` are road and bridge points, which the terrain draws).
- Which entries become objects, as startNewGame's loop (`0x004a17c3` on) decides: templates hold KindOf as four words at `+0x68`. Bridges (`+0x6c` bit `0x800000`, or the byte at `+0x228`) always do. Otherwise skipped: shrubbery (`+0x68` bit `0x40`) unless GlobalData `+0x10` (use trees), optimized trees (`+0x74` bit `0x40`, they go to the tree buffer), props (`+0x74` bit `0x20`, the prop buffer), and clutter (`+0x6c` bit `0x4000` with fence width `+0x1cc` 0.0) when it's turned into props: unless `[0x00a39844]+0xa00` is 2, or 3 with GlobalData `+0x52`. A multiplayer recording (TheRecorder `0x00a39914`, `0x0048e3a0`) forces both switches on.
- Each one gets a drawable the way the loop makes one for a tree: TheThingFactory (`0x00a39b24`) newDrawable `0x004adc00` (thiscall: template, status 0), setOrientation `0x005442b0` (angle), setPosition `0x00476700` (location with z plus TerrainLogic `0x00a39594` vtable `+0x14` getGroundHeight(x, y, 0)), and after the draw TheGameClient (`0x00a39b18`) vtable `+0x4c` destroyDrawable, which deletes at once. A drawable starts its ambient sound when made outside a map load, so the loading flag is held at 1 while ours are made. GlobalData `+0x20c` (make track marks) is held at 0 too: W3DModelDraw::setModelState binds terrain tracks (`+0x5c`) when it's set (`0x007b4839`), and its reactToTransformChange `0x007b5c50` then reads the drawable's Object (`+0x88`, then `+0x80`) with no null check, which crashed the first version at the shell map on a map-placed vehicle. Drawables only use the client's random numbers (EA `Drawable.cpp`, `W3DModelDraw.cpp`), so making them on one machine doesn't desync multiplayer.
- Night: GameClient::setTimeOfDay `0x004aa430` calls Drawable::setTimeOfDay `0x00476ca0` on every drawable at map load, which a new drawable never gets. It sets bit `0x80` of the condition flags at `+0x118` and marks the model dirty (`+0x14c`). We set the bit and apply it the way the game does (`0x00476462`): each draw module in the null-terminated array at `+0xc0`, vtable `+0x50` for its draw interface, then that one's `+0x2c` with the flags, unless `[0x00a39840]` is above 0. GlobalData `+0x204` is the time of day, 4 is night.
- Drawing: the models (each W3DModelDraw adds its render object to the scene, user data a DrawableInfo with the drawable at `+4` and flags at `+0xc`) are found on RTS3DScene's (vtable `0x009687b4`) render list: head at `+0x3c`, a node's next at `+4` and its object's list link at `+0xc`, `0x10` into the render object. Each one of ours goes through RTS3DScene::renderOneObject `0x00766d30` (thiscall: render info, render object, local player index from ThePlayerList `0x00a395a0` `+0xc` `+0x24`), as Customized_Render `0x00767e30` does: it builds the light environment from the scene's lights on the stack, sets render info `+0x1c` to it, renders, and clears it, queueing nothing. A drawable without an Object counts as clear of the shroud.
- Trees: DoTrees `0x0074fc10` (cdecl, render info) draws the terrain's tree buffer with the render info's camera.
- Order: terrain, trees, the models, a flush, water, a flush, so the water goes over what stands in it.
- Not done: the owner's house color (a map object of a player shows the default color), damage states, and snow variants.

## The shroud (fog of war on the radar)

- W3DRadar vtable `0x009692c0`: `+0x24` draw `0x0077b8d0`, `+0x28` clearShroud `0x0077b6f0`, `+0x2c` setShroudLevel `0x0077b750` (thiscall, cell x, cell y, setting, callee pops 12), refreshTerrain `0x0077bc30` (entry `0x009692d8`), newMap (entry `0x009692e0`).
- Shroud cells are W3DShroud's (TheTerrainRenderObject `0x00a48b30` `+0x2844`), cell width and height at `+0x10`/`+0x14`, one terrain cell (10 units). setShroudLevel turns the cell into a world rectangle, then into radar texels through worldToRadar `0x00496d70` (both ends inclusive), and fills them in the 128x128 shroud texture (`+0x1370`, A8R8G8B8) with black at alpha 255 (setting 2, shrouded), 127 (1, fogged) or 0. On a 400 cell map a radar texel covers about three cells, so the game's own data is finer than its radar shows.
- Ours ("Minimap fog resolution", 128 to 1024, default 512): both vtable entries go to wrappers that run the game's code and then write the same cell into a texture of ours of that size (the texels whose centers lie in the cell, or the one under its center when the cell is smaller than a texel), which the device draws in place of the game's (a pinned `replacement`, kept while the game keeps writing its own). Starting in the middle of a match it is filled from the game's 128x128 one first.
- At any resolution the edges still step from cell to cell, since a cell is all the game knows (in game at 512 the result looked like the same fog with smaller steps). A "Minimap fog edges" setting drew it through the world fog's smoothing as well, and showed no visible difference in game, so it was removed.

## The unit dots and the camera box

- W3DRadar::draw redraws the overlay (unit dots) when `TheGameClient->getFrame() % 6 == 0` (`0x0077baa5`), clearing it and calling renderObjectList `0x0077a4a0` (thiscall, list, texture, hero flag) for the object list (`+0x10`) and the local one (`+0x14`). renderObjectList puts each dot at position / (map size × `[0x009476d8]` = 1/128) and plots 2x2 pixels with four SurfaceClass::DrawPixel `0x00852c40` calls (thiscall, x, y, color, callee pops 12; surface at SurfaceClass `+0xc`), each behind `cmp reg, 0x80` limits. Stealthed units blink through their color's alpha.
- Ours ("Minimap units and view", sharp by default): the overlay TextureClass (`+0x1364`) gets a 512x512 texture of ours in place of its own (swapped at `+0x38` while the setting is on, the game's kept to put back), the two 1/128 operands point at our 1/512, the eight limits become 512, and each of the four DrawPixel calls writes a 4x4 block (offset by which of the four it is), so a dot keeps its size but sits on a grid four times finer. The redraw check became a call to us: every 6 client frames as before, or with sharp dots once per logic step.
- drawViewBox `0x00779550` (thiscall, x, y, width, height, callee pops 16, called from `0x0077bc1a`) draws the camera box from corners that reconstructViewBox `0x007792b0` turned into whole radar cells (TheTacticalView vtable `+0x2c`, the four screen corners at the radar's average height `+0x18`), cached until the zoom or angle changes, so the box moved in steps of a cell. Lines clipped to the radar window (`+0x1330`, winGetSize `0x004f91e0`, winGetScreenPosition `0x004f90f0`), TheDisplay `+0x80` drawLine and `+0x7c` with two colors, `0xffe1e100` on top fading to `0xff9e9e00` at the bottom. Ours, with sharp units: the same lines from the four corners every frame, in exact pixels.

## The fog of war in the world

- W3DShroud keeps the fog as a TextureClass at `+0x28` (one texel per cell plus a border, R5G6B5), copied from its source every frame with CopyRects, and the terrain samples it through a projection (the shader setup `0x0074e4e0` scales by `1 / (cells × cell size)`). Bilinear filtering on a texture that coarse leaves square cell edges.
- Ours ("Fog of war edges", smooth by default, `fog.rs`): every frame the texture is marked `smooth`, and when it changed the device draws it four times larger (at most 2048 square) through a cubic B-spline (`post::SMOOTH_UPSCALE`, four bilinear taps, all four channels) into an 8-bit image that draws then sample in its place. Same coordinates, so nothing else changes. Covered by the GPU test: a hard edge between two texels comes out as a gradient, in color and in alpha. The first version made the smoothed image as an opaque one, which reads alpha as 1, and the alpha check caught it.
- The smoothed copy is redrawn when the texture was written since the last redraw (`changed_since_smooth`, set by every write). It used to go by the dirty bits at draw time, but Clear uploads every bound texture and clears them. The game writes the fog with CopyRects right before Begin_Render's Clear, so whenever the fog texture was still bound from the last draw, the change was lost and the fog froze. Seen in Generals' USA06 after the map expanded: newly revealed ground stayed black, and after a reload everything explored before the load was right while later reveals stayed black. With "Fog of war edges" set to the game default it was correct. The GPU test writes the texture, clears with it bound, and draws.

### The fog on objects

- W3DShroud fields (same in both builds): cell counts `+0`, `+4`, cell size `+0x10`, `+0x14` (floats), source surface `+0x1c`, its data `+0x20` (one R5G6B5 value per cell) and row pitch in bytes `+0x24`. setShroudLevel (Zero Hour `0x00769740`, Generals `0x00749c10`, thiscall x, y, level byte) skips cells past the counts and writes the shroud color scaled by the level, raised to at least the shroud alpha (GlobalData Zero Hour `+0x8f6`, Generals `+0x8ee`). Level 255 is written as white, `0xFFFF`, so any other value darkens the ground. The border fill is Zero Hour `0x007698d0`, Generals `0x00749f20`. A cell's source position is `floor(world / cell size)` in both builds: the texture is the source moved one texel for the border (and in Generals by the draw window's origin, which the shader setup takes back off).
- RTS3DScene::renderOneObject (Zero Hour `0x00766d30`, Generals `0x007465c0`, EA's W3DScene.cpp) works out the object's shroud status (`[esp+0x14]`, 1 is clear) and at the end, in the default pass mode (scene Zero Hour `+0x58c`, Generals `+0x4d8`), renders it plainly when the status is at most 1 (`cmp dword [esp+0x14], 1; jle`, Zero Hour `0x0076737b`, Generals `0x00746c0a`). Otherwise it pushes the shroud material pass (scene Zero Hour `+0x538`, Generals `+0x488`), which multiplies the object by the fog texture projected from above, renders, and pops it. The render object is `[esp+0x29c]` (Zero Hour) or `[esp+0x248]` (Generals) there. Its Get_Bounding_Sphere is vtable Zero Hour `+0xf0`, Generals `+0xf4` (world center and radius).
- The status comes from the logic cells under the object's footprint (PartitionData::getShroudedStatus, EA source). Cells off the partition grid (outside the active boundary, which is all the grid covers) are skipped, and the footprint can be smaller than the model, so an object hanging into dark ground can count as clear and be drawn at full brightness. Seen in Generals' first China mission with buildings along the dark edge.
- Ours ("Fog of war on objects", like the ground by default, `fog.rs`): the compare becomes a call that compares our status instead. For a clear object we read the source cells under its bounding sphere (center ± radius); if any isn't `0xFFFF`, or the box leaves the source or spans more than 128 cells, the status becomes 2 (partially clear) and the game's own shroud pass draws it. The logic's status isn't touched, so nothing the simulation sees changes. Clear objects next to the fog's edge get the pass too, and the smoothed fog darkens their side facing it, as it does the ground.
- Ghosts (what the player last saw of a structure now in the fog: drawable info with no drawable, status set to 3 at Zero Hour `0x00766de0`, Generals `0x00746670`) never reach that compare. A block early on (Zero Hour `0x00767143`, Generals `0x007469f9`) sets the RenderInfoClass light environment (`+0x1c`) to the scene's fogged lights (Zero Hour `+0x314`, Generals `+0x2bc`), renders (render object vtable Zero Hour `+0x24`, Generals `+0x2c`), clears it and jumps to the end. So rubble and other ghosts were only dimmed, never darkened by the fog texture, and stayed visible over shrouded or off-map ground. Seen as train cars and rubble in Generals' first China mission.
- updateFixedLightEnvironments (Zero Hour `0x00767500`, Generals `0x00746d90`, EA source) builds the scene's default lights at `+0xf0` (the scene's ambient and global lights) and the fogged lights from them scaled by fog alpha / clear alpha (GlobalData Zero Hour `+0x8f5` / `+0x8f4`, Generals `+0x8ed` / `+0x8ec`). The fog texture holds the same fog alpha in fogged cells, so normal lights times the fog texture come out about as dark as the fogged lights.
- Ours: the ghost block is replaced by a call (`render_ghost`, with the scene, render object and RenderInfoClass) and a jump to the end. With "Fog of war on objects" on, the default pass mode, and the cells under the ghost's bounding sphere not all one value (a fog edge, or past the fog's cells), it renders with the default lights and the shroud material pass (Push_Material_Pass Zero Hour `0x008580d0`, Generals `0x008234e0`, Pop_Material_Pass `0x00858100`, `0x00823510`), so it fades into the shroud like the ground. Otherwise it does what the game did. Ghosts wholly in the fog keep the game's look and cost no extra draw.
